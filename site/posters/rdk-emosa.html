1A pod joins

From power-on to an EasyMesh agent with the controller's networks, about two minutes.

  1. The pod boots onto its bootstrap uplink: its station joins em-gtp's opensync-lab-bhaul (5 GHz, channel 44).
  2. em-gtp leases it a 169.254.2.x address; OpenSync builds a gretap to .1, which em-gtp bridges with eth1 into the wired LAN, a port of the gateway's brlan0.
  3. OpenSync dials its redirector, 10.101.0.40:6640, over the LAN and the gateway's WAN. The operator's redirect answers there, once (a stand-in on the VM, plan 5.3): manager_addr is the gateway's front port, tcp:10.0.0.1:6640.
  4. EMOSA's forwarder in the gateway carries the pod to the fleet on loopback. The fleet admits it, records it on /nvram, starts its agent, writes manager_addr tcp:10.0.0.1:6651…6690 and hangs up; the pod reconnects, through the forwarder, to its agent.
  5. The agent opens its 1905 socket on its macvlan in namespace emosa and onboards: AP-Autoconfiguration Search and Response, M1, M2.
  6. It writes the M2's networks to the pod in one guarded OVSDB transaction, counted applied once the pod's State shows them.
  7. Option 1: it moves the pod's station to the gateway's mesh_backhaul (02:00:00:89:2b:59) as a 4-address Multi-AP station, applied within 90 s.

2EasyMesh, both ways

Each agent speaks for its pod; RDK's controller sees eight agents.

  1. Topology: the pod's radios, BSSes and clients, and its backhaul station with its parent BSSID.
  2. Metrics: periodic AP metrics and link metrics, made from the pod's own statistics.
  3. Client steering: a steering request becomes a BTM request through the pod's steering window.
  4. Backhaul steering: the controller moves a pod's station to another backhaul BSS; the target is kept for the pod's later starts.
  5. Renewal: an agent that hears no Topology Query for 120 s onboards again, as after a controller restart.

3The pods' data

Two ways into the home LAN. Every OpenSync restart lands a pod on the first.

  1. GRE through em-gtp, the baseline: a 3-address station on opensync-lab-bhaul, one gretap per pod lease, bridged into the wired LAN and so into brlan0. em-gtp keeps the underlay out of the LAN.
  2. The EasyMesh Wi-Fi backhaul, option 1: a 4-address station on a mesh_backhaul BSS, bridged into the pod's br-home, no GRE. The controller can move it between the gateway's and the extenders' backhaul BSSes.
  3. A switch not applied in 90 s holds the pod on GRE until it is admitted anew.

4The pods' statistics

OpenSync's own telemetry, turned into EasyMesh for the controller and the optimizer.

  1. Each pod's qm publishes client reports and a channel survey every 5 s to 10.0.0.1:8883: MQTT over mutual TLS, the pod's device certificate and the broker's both from the lab's CA (the operator's side, on the VM).
  2. The broker is the gateway image's mosquitto. Each agent subscribes to its pod's topic on the gateway's own loopback, 127.0.0.1:1883.
  3. The agent reports them as EasyMesh: AP metrics, associated and unassociated station link metrics.
  4. The optimizer reads them from the controller like any agent's, and steers the pods' clients too.
